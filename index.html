My Mazes

    How a maze grows

    Every maze starts as a grid of tiny rooms with walls all around. A bright spark builds the maze by knocking down walls, one at a time.

    The steps

    1. Close every door. At the start, every square has a wall on all four sides.
    2. Start at S. The bright spark begins on the Start square.
    3. Pick a new square. The spark looks at the squares next to it that it has not visited yet. It picks one by chance and knocks down the wall in between.
    4. Keep digging. It does this again and again, making a long tunnel.
    5. Back up at a dead end. When there is no new square next to it, the spark walks back along its tunnel. It stops at the first square that still has a new neighbour, and digs a new branch from there.
    6. Stop when every square is visited. Now every room is joined to the rest of the maze.
    Grown-up words

    This way of building is called depth-first search. Depth first means the spark goes as deep as it can before it turns around. Walking back is called backtracking.

    How it checks the maze is real

    • No locked rooms. The spark visits every square, so there is always a way from S to F.
    • Checked before you see it. The computer builds the whole maze in secret first. Then it measures the way from S to F. If the trip is too short, it throws that maze away and tries again. What you watch is a replay of a maze that passed the test.
    • S and F stay apart. If S and F are next to each other, the wall between them is never knocked down. So you always have to go the long way round.

    Maze codes

    When the spark picks a square “by chance”, the chances all come from one secret number, called the seed. The same seed always makes the same choices, so it grows the same maze. Every maze has a code under Maze code. Its letters and numbers pack in the seed, the size, the style, the shape and where S and F are. Type or paste a code in, tap Grow, and the very same maze grows again. Share link sends the maze as a link: opening it grows the very same maze for whoever taps it. Codes are printed under the maze, too.

    Easy, Medium and Hard

    Each level picks a size and sets three dials. You can move the dials yourself under Maze style.

    Twisty
    How often the tunnels turn. Straight tunnels are easier to follow.
    Shortcuts
    Extra holes in walls, so there is more than one way through. Shortcuts that would make the trip too short get put back.
    Branches
    A few long dead ends, or lots of short side paths.

    Easy is small with shortcuts. Medium is the classic maze. Hard is big and very twisty.

    Shapes

    Pick a picture under Shape, like a cat or a rocket, and the maze grows inside it. The outline of the picture is the outside wall, so the spark only digs inside the shape. S and F start far apart, and you can still move them anywhere inside. Shapes need a grid of 15 × 15 or bigger, so there are enough squares to draw the picture.

    How the search finds the way

    When you tap Discover the Path, the computer hunts for the shortest way from S to F. It can’t see the whole maze at once like you can. So it spreads out like a ripple in a pond.

    The numbers show how many steps each square is from S.

    The steps

    1. Start at S. S is 0 steps away.
    2. Look one step out. Find every square you can reach in one step without crossing a wall. Those squares are 1 step away.
    3. Spread the wave. From those, find the squares 2 steps away, then 3, then 4. The wave grows one step at a time, in every direction at once. It flows around walls, just like water.
    4. Stop at F. As soon as the wave touches F, the search is done.
    5. Follow the trail back. Every square remembers which square the wave came from. The computer follows those clues from F back to S. That is the gold path.
    Why is gold the shortest?

    The wave reaches every square 1 step away before any square 2 steps away. So the very first time it touches F, it has used the fewest steps possible.

    Grown-up words

    This is called breadth-first search, or BFS. Breadth means width. It searches wide, like a ripple, before it goes far.

    Colours you will see

    • Frontier is the edge of the wave: the next squares to check.
    • Explored squares were already checked. They keep their colour so you can see every place the search tried.
    • Shortest path is the one route the computer keeps.

    Tap a colour in the key above the maze to pick your own colour for it.

    The numbers

    Explored
    How many squares the search has checked.
    Frontier
    How many squares are on the edge of the wave right now.
    Distance
    How many steps from S the wave has spread.
    Shortest
    How many steps the gold path takes from S to F.

    Building goes deep: one long tunnel at a time. Searching goes wide: a ripple in every direction. If walls block every way to F, the wave runs out of squares and the search tells you there is no way through.

    Solve it yourself

    Can you find the way? Use your Apple Pencil or your finger to draw a line from S to F.

    How to play

    1. Tap Solve It Yourself.
    2. Put your Pencil or finger on S.
    3. Draw to F. Stay in the open paths. You can’t go through walls or jump over squares.
    4. Lifted by mistake? No problem. Your line waits for you, but the clock keeps going. Touch the end of your line to keep going.
    5. Wrong way? Go back along your line and it rubs out.

    Race your ghost

    Play a maze you have finished before and a little ghost races you. It is your best run on that maze, played back exactly as you drew it, wrong turns and all. It sets off when your line leaves S. Get to F first and your run becomes the new ghost. Tap the ghost button above the maze to hide it.

    Your score

    Time
    How long you took, from leaving S to reaching F. The clock keeps going while your Pencil is lifted.
    Cells
    Every square you step on, starting with S. Going back counts too, because you still walked there.
    Best
    The fewest squares it takes to get from S to F. The computer finds this with its search wave before you start.
    Efficiency
    Best divided by Cells. Walk the shortest way and you get 100%: a Perfect Route!
    Backtracks
    How many times you went back. That’s fine. Every explorer does it!
    For example

    Best is 20 and your Cells count is 25. 20 ÷ 25 = 0.8, so your efficiency is 80%. Try again and see if you can reach 100%!
